Privacy

Short version: we don't want your information, we don't ask for it, and we couldn't hand it over even if someone made us, because we never have it.

Nothing you answer here is ever sent anywhere.

Wish Granted runs entirely in your browser. When you answer a question, that answer is held in memory on your device, used to figure out which programs to show you, and then it's gone — closing or reloading the tab erases it. There is no server that receives your answers, because the app never sends them. There's nothing to opt out of, because nothing is being collected.

What we don't have

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no email address collected.
  • No cookies, and nothing written to your browser's storage.
  • No analytics script, no ad tracker, and nothing that identifies you or follows you from one visit to the next. The hosting service's own dashboard does show us a running total of requests to the site — a single aggregate number, not something we installed or can turn off, and it says nothing about who visited, where from, or what anyone answered.
  • Your answers never appear in the page's web address, so they can't end up in your browser history or in a link you accidentally share.

You don't have to take our word for any of this. This site sends a Content-Security-Policy header that refuses, at the browser level, to let this page open a connection to send data anywhere — no background request of any kind (the technical terms are fetch, XHR, WebSocket), not even one back to us. That's what actually enforces "your answers never leave your browser": not us choosing not to send anything, but the browser refusing to let the page try. You can check it yourself in developer tools, under Network or Application → Security — look for connect-src 'none' in the policy. (You will still see this page's own script and stylesheet load when you first open it; that's normal, same-origin, and the one thing the policy does allow.)

What the site you're on necessarily sees

This is true of every website, not just this one, and it isn't something we collect — it's how the web works. To send you this page at all, the infrastructure that hosts it (Cloudflare) has to process your IP address, the time of your visit, and which page you requested, the same as any postal service has to read the address on an envelope to deliver it.

We do not run our own server and we have no dashboard, log file, or export that shows us that information. On the hosting plan we use, we were never given access to raw visit logs in the first place. We think that's the right way to build this: we can't see what we were never given, we can't be compelled to hand over something we don't have, and there's no log of yours sitting somewhere for us to lose or mishandle.

That said, we're being precise, not evasive: Cloudflare, as the infrastructure we run on, still processes that connection information on its own systems in order to operate its network, under its own privacy policy — which we don't control and don't have visibility into. If you want the specifics of how long Cloudflare itself retains that operational data, see Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Links to other sites

Every program shown links to the government or agency page it was written from, so you can confirm it yourself. Those are links to other organizations' websites, which have their own privacy practices we don't control. We do send those links without a referrer, so the site you land on has no way to see that you came from a benefits-screening tool.
